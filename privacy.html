Privacy Policy

This is not a real legal document. Claroni is a fictional company and this site is a practice build, made to learn on. Nothing written here creates any obligation, no service is provided, and no money can change hands — the payment flow runs in Stripe test mode, where real cards are declined. Do not rely on any of it.

Last updated: 18 August 2026

Claroni sees into your money, so it is fair that you know exactly what we do with your data. This document says it plainly: what we collect, why, who else gets to see it, how long we keep it, and what you can do about it. We run no analytics and no advertising trackers.

1. Who is the controller

No controller exists. Claroni is a fictional company and nobody operates this site, so no personal data is in fact being processed here. The rest of this document describes how it would work if the service ran — and it is written from what the code actually does, not from a template.

Processing is governed by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the Czech Personal Data Processing Act.

2. What data we process

We collect only what the service cannot work without. This is the complete list.

Account
Your email address and password (stored as an irreversible hash, never in readable form). If you sign in with Google, we also receive the name on your Google account.
Subscription
Identifiers from Stripe, the status of your subscription, your next renewal date, and whether it is set to cancel at the end of the period. No card details are among them.
Financial data
Transactions and balances from the accounts and cards you choose to connect, plus the categories the app sorts them into. The access is read-only.
Newsletter
If you subscribe, we store only your email address and the date you signed up. Nothing else.
Technical data
Our providers keep operational logs containing IP address and browser type. They are used to run and secure the service, not to track you.

What we do not collect: national identification numbers, ID document numbers, your location, your contacts, or anything else from your device beyond the above. And as section 4 explains, we run no analytics.

3. Why, and on what legal basis

The GDPR requires us to state a legal basis for each purpose. Here they all are.

Providing the service
Running your account, showing you your finances, managing your subscription. Basis: performance of a contract, Article 6(1)(b) GDPR.
Bank connection
Reading transactions from the accounts you connect. Basis: your explicit consent, Article 6(1)(a) GDPR, which you can withdraw at any time.
Newsletter
Sending updates. Basis: your consent. Every email has an unsubscribe link.
Accounting and tax
Keeping records of payments. Basis: compliance with a legal obligation, Article 6(1)(c) GDPR.
Security
Operational logs and abuse prevention. Basis: our legitimate interest, Article 6(1)(f) GDPR, in keeping the service working and unabused.

We do not sell your data to anyone. We do not use it for advertising, or for profiling with legal effects for you. We make no automated decisions about you.

4. Cookies and browser storage

Claroni sets no cookies. We have no Google Analytics, no Meta pixel, and no other analytics or advertising tool. That is why you will not find a cookie consent banner on this site — there would be nothing to consent to.

We do use browser storage (localStorage), for these three things:

  • claroni-theme — whether you have light or dark mode on;
  • your language choice — whether you read the site in English or Czech;
  • a sign-in token from Supabase — this is what keeps you logged in instead of asking for your password on every page. If you signed in with Google, a Google token is stored alongside it.

All of this is strictly necessary to run the site and keep you signed in, so it does not require consent under the law. You can clear it yourself at any time by deleting site data in your browser settings, or by signing out.

One more thing it is only fair to state: our pages load fonts from Google Fonts and part of our code from the esm.sh library. Each page load sends your IP address to those services. They are not tracking you on our behalf and we send them nothing about you, but technically it happens and you should know.

5. Payments

Subscription payments are processed by Stripe. When you pay, you enter your card details directly into Stripe’s form — they do not pass through our servers and we never see your card number.

From Stripe we store only a customer and subscription identifier, the payment status, and the renewal date. That is all we need in order to know which plan you are on.

In Manage billing, which you will find on your dashboard, you can change your card, download invoices, or cancel at any time. That page is operated by Stripe directly.

Stripe is an independent controller of the data it processes for payment services and fraud prevention. Its own policy is at stripe.com/privacy.

6. Who we share data with

We sell your data to nobody. We pass it only to the suppliers the service cannot run without, and only as far as necessary. This is the complete list.

Supabase
Database and authentication. Your accounts, subscriptions and newsletter signups are stored here.
Stripe
Payment processing, invoicing and the customer portal.
Vercel
Website hosting. Keeps operational logs containing visitors’ IP addresses.
Google (sign-in and fonts)
Google sign-in, if you use it. Separately, fonts are loaded from Google Fonts, which sends your IP address.
esm.sh
A library service from which part of our browser code is loaded. It receives your IP address and nothing else.

We may otherwise disclose data only where the law requires it, or where a public authority requests it on a valid legal basis.

7. Transfers outside the EU

Some of the suppliers above are based in the United States, so your data may leave the European Economic Area.

Where that happens, the transfer relies on Standard Contractual Clauses approved by the European Commission, or on an adequacy decision where the EU-US Data Privacy Framework applies. We will provide a copy of the clauses on request.

8. How long we keep data

Account data
For as long as you have an account with us. After you delete it, we remove them within 30 days.
Financial data
Until you disconnect the account or delete your profile. We then delete them within 30 days.
Payment records
Ten years from the end of the tax period, as accounting law requires. We cannot shorten this even at your request.
Newsletter
Until you unsubscribe.
Operational logs
Typically up to 30 days, depending on each supplier’s settings.

9. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the following rights against us. Exercising them is free — just write to the contact in section 13.

  • Access: you can ask what data we hold about you and receive a copy of it.
  • Rectification: we must correct anything inaccurate.
  • Erasure: you can ask us to delete your data, except what we are legally required to keep.
  • Restriction: you can ask us to pause processing while something is being resolved.
  • Portability: we will hand over the data you gave us in a machine-readable format.
  • Objection: you can object at any time to processing based on legitimate interest.
  • Withdraw consent: wherever we rely on consent, you can withdraw it at any time. Processing up to that point remains lawful.

We reply within one month. If it will take longer, we will tell you why.

10. Security

All traffic between your browser and our servers is encrypted. Passwords are stored as an irreversible hash, so not even we know them. Database access is restricted by row-level rules, so only you can reach your own data.

No security is absolute. If a breach occurred that posed a high risk to you, we will tell you without undue delay and report it to the Czech Data Protection Authority within 72 hours, as the GDPR requires.

11. Children

Claroni is intended for people aged 18 and over, and we do not knowingly process children’s data. If you find that a child has given us data, write to us and we will delete it.

12. Changes to this policy

This policy may change when we add a feature or swap a supplier. The last-updated date is always at the top. We will email you in advance about any substantial change, particularly a new purpose of processing.

13. Contact and complaints

There is nowhere to write, and nobody to write to. The email in the footer is invented. A real service would give a working contact here, and a data protection officer if the law required one.

If you think we are handling your data badly, tell us first — most things can be put right quickly. You also have the right to complain to the supervisory authority: Úřad pro ochranu osobních údajů, Pplk. Sochora 27, 170 00 Prague 7, uoou.gov.cz.
